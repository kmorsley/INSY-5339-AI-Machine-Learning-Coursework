## Overview 

This assignment explores decision-tree models for two business prediction tasks using a bank-customer dataset:

1. **Classification:** Predict whether a customer will accept a loan offer (`Loan_Accepted`).
2. **Regression:** Predict annual revenue generated by a customer (`Annual_Bank_Revenue_K`).

The analysis focuses on model interpretability, Gini versus entropy splitting criteria, tree depth, overfitting, pre-pruning, feature importance, and evaluation of predictive performance.

## Dataset

**Source workbook:** `Homework7_Classification_Regression_Trees_Data.xlsx`  
**Worksheets:** `Customer_Data` and `Data_Dictionary`  
**Observations:** 650 bank customers

Both models use the same nine predictors: age, annual income, credit score, family size, education level, account tenure, average monthly card spending, digital engagement score, and product count. `Customer_ID` is retained for identification only and is excluded from modeling. Neither target variable is included among the predictors.

## Methods

### Classification trees

- Partition data into 80% training and 20% testing sets using `random_state=42` and stratification by loan acceptance.
- Fit a baseline decision tree with Gini impurity.
- Evaluate training accuracy and testing accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and confusion matrix.
- Compare Gini and entropy criteria with a controlled maximum depth of five.
- Evaluate maximum depths of 3, 5, 7, and unrestricted to examine overfitting and identify a practical pre-pruned model.
- Visualize the selected tree, extract its decision rules, and analyze feature importance.

### Regression trees

- Create a separate 80% training / 20% testing split using `random_state=42`, without stratification.
- Compare regression trees with maximum depths of 3, 5, 7, and unrestricted.
- Evaluate training and testing R², testing RMSE, node counts, and leaf counts.
- Select a practical model and compare actual with predicted annual customer revenue.

### Business applications

Use the selected classification and regression trees to predict loan acceptance and annual bank revenue for three new customer scenarios (A, B, and C).
